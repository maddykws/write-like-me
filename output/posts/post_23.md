The question I ask before every AI feature my team builds: what happens when it's wrong?

Not if. When. Every model is wrong sometimes. The teams that ship safely are the ones who designed for that day one.

What I push for as a manager:
- A graceful fallback when confidence is low
- A human in the loop where the cost of error is high
- Clear UX that sets expectations instead of overpromising

Great AI products aren't the ones that are never wrong. They're the ones that fail gracefully when they are.
