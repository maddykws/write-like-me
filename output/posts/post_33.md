A 30% reduction in tokens isn't a cost story to my finance team. It's a latency story to my users. As a manager, I sell it as both.

Fewer tokens in and out usually means a faster response. So token optimization quietly improves the thing users actually feel.

How I frame the work to get it prioritized:
- To finance: this lowers our cost per user
- To product: this makes the feature feel faster
- To engineers: this is a craft problem worth solving

The same work, told three ways, gets buy-in from three audiences. That translation is the manager's job.
